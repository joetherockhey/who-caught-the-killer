Who caught the killer?

The Mentalist — Season 1

Patrick Jane is a consultant with no badge and no gun. The CBI detectives do the records, the forensics and the arrests. So who actually catches the killer? I am scoring all 23 episodes, in order. Every take of every episode is kept here.

The forensics named the wrong man

The detectives’ twenty per cent is real work: they confirmed the copycat staging against old case footage, and one of them made the arrest Jane physically could not. But their one big lead — the hair in the taunting note — was planted by the killer for them to find. Jane faked a diary, lifted a keycard during a hug, and let his own team chase an innocent man to do it.

Red Hair and Silver Tape — a teenager taped and dumped in a vineyard. My analysis: Jane 55%, the detectives 45%.

Much closer than episode one, and that is the point

Jane names the chef on no evidence at all — his stated reason is that the man uses too much butter — and he never works out the wife. His two flashy tricks, the hypnosis and the invented handwriting expert, each produce an innocent man. His one decisive read is of a timeline Lisbon built, off a pattern a detective found in the database. Lisbon kicks the door and fires the shots.

He took her; his wife killed her

The cut that made it work. Jane accuses the chef of taking her, not of killing her — which is all the evidence he actually has — so the last line completes the accusation instead of reversing it. A clarity test that had graded three earlier versions loses the viewer passed this one: “the accusation holds, the twist completes instead of reversing, and a viewer carries three names for two minutes.”
